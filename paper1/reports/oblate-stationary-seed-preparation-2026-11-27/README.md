## 1. A stationary solution would satisfy the complete linearized system

Use the same static oblate background, $\rho=r^2+\epsilon$, $\Sigma=r^2+\epsilon x^2$, and $N=3$, $m=0$. Let the first-order shifts and canonical momenta vanish:
$$
s_1=(b_1,W_1)=0,\qquad p_1=0,\qquad \partial_t q_1=\partial_t a_1=0.
$$
The momentum map holds because the spatial velocity and shift both vanish. The remaining stationary linearized equations are
$$
Lq_1=0,\qquad V''q_1+L^*a_1=0.
$$
These are the original bulk differential expressions, with compact radial tests. A smooth solution throughout an open radial interval gives a stationary retained linearized solution there. On the regular Legendre patch, the momentum secondaries, lapse tertiaries, Hamilton equations, and their preservation follow from these equations and stationarity. Merely satisfying them at one radius or for finitely many time/radial jets does not suffice.

The direct alternative is to check every retained linearized coefficient Euler equation. It avoids treating a weak inverse of one reduced bracket as a complete solution. The prepared exporter forms the full four-dimensional $\delta G_{\mu\nu}$ and all 22 retained axisymmetric coefficient tests, including temporal tests.

Equatorial reflection reduces the relevant even-under-$\phi\mapsto-\phi$ seed to
$$
u=(\lambda_0,\lambda_2,X_0,X_2,V_{\mathrm p,2},C_{\mathrm p,2},a_0,a_2).
$$
This contains six spatial and two lapse profiles. Equatorially odd polar seeds cannot contribute to the degree-four response used below. Eight retained rows are potentially nonzero; this is a symmetry selection, not an independent-constraint count. All 22 original rows remain in the export so that the other 14 must be checked to vanish.

The radial base transformation generated by $F(r)\partial_r$ changes $X_0$ by $2rF$. On a shell away from zero it can set $X_0=0$. The prepared exporter deliberately keeps $X_0$ and its equation. Any later gauge-fixed construction must satisfy the original unreduced rows as well.

## 2. A known odd coordinate mode supplies the other parity

For a smooth radial profile $f$, the first-order continuum coordinate variation generated by
$$

$$

$$

$$
Call this odd perturbation $g_f$. Its full linearized Einstein residual is zero. The $f'$ term is essential to that statement; it is not discarded or frozen.

For the tested descriptor $\xi=w(r)x\partial_\phi$, the passed eight-channel map gives
$$

=\frac{8\rho wf}{35}\,e_{X,4}
-\frac{4\rho wf}{105}\,e_{C_{\mathrm p},4}.
$$
Here $e_{X,4}$ and $e_{C_{\mathrm p},4}$ are unit coefficient tests. The axial radial vector contributes no discarded test in this particular expression because its $x$ component is zero.

For an admissible stationary polar seed $e$, reflection in $\phi$ kills its self-pairing, and $E_{\mathrm{full}}^{(1)}[g_f]=0$. Thus
$$
\boxed{B_\xi[e+g_f]
=\frac4{105}\int dr\,\rho\,wf\,D[e],\qquad
D[e]=6\mathscr E_{X,4}^{(1)}[e]
-\mathscr E_{C_{\mathrm p},4}^{(1)}[e].}
$$
The $\mathscr E$ are coefficient Euler densities after angular averaging. Their definition already includes the single factor $\Sigma$ and the off-diagonal contraction factors:
$$
\mathscr E_A^{(1)}[e](r)
=-\frac12\int_{-1}^{1}dx\,
\Sigma\,\delta G^{\mu\nu}[e]\,Dg[e_A]_{\mu\nu}.
$$
There is no additional radial volume factor in the boxed formula.

This use of a gauge odd leg does not make the entire mixed perturbation a gauge control. A nonzero $D[e]$ requires a nonzero full continuum Einstein residual for $e$, which is sufficient to exclude a pure continuum-gauge interpretation at the flat background. Conversely, a full continuum solution also has zero residual and zero $B$, whether or not it is pure gauge.

The known linearized accidental symmetry does not by itself force $D[e]=0$. A hidden identity could still force this row to vanish on every stationary solution. It must be tested through the same radial reduction as the stationary equations. If it vanishes on a chosen subfamily only, the conclusion concerns that subfamily.

## 3. Local germ, not a new physical boundary model

For this necessary bulk test, it is sufficient to have a smooth stationary solution on an open shell and to choose $f,w$ with compact support inside that shell. No physical endpoint conditions or null-boundary quantum theory are selected. This local solution-germ specification is a separate admissibility route from a globally realized canonical boundary domain; it does not claim to provide that domain.

An explicit way to certify a genuine family is to construct smooth matrices $M(r),K(r)$ with
$$
u(r)=M(r)z(r),\qquad z'=K(r)z.
$$
Substitute
$$
u'=(M'+MK)z,\qquad
u''=(M''+2M'K+MK'+MK^2)z
$$
into every original retained row. Exact zero identities throughout an interval, together with smooth coefficients, provide actual local solutions of the linear ODE for allowed initial values. The same substitution gives a response row $D[e]=d(r)z(r)$.

If $d(r_0)z_0\ne0$ for some allowed initial value, continuity supplies a smaller interval with nonzero response. Compact $f,w$ can then make the boxed integral nonzero. This would exclude a sufficiently smooth exact retained solution family with the **specified mixed tangent** $e+g_f$. It would not exclude the static oblate background, prove an obstruction for $e$ alone, or classify every representative of a linearized gauge class.

No $M,K,z_0$ are supplied by the present preparation. A complete differential-algebraic reduction is one route to finding them, but a verified responding subfamily would suffice. A finite jet, a nullspace at one radius, or an approximate finite radial Galerkin solution is not this certificate.

$$

=\frac1{2r^2(k-1)\rho^{k-1}}
